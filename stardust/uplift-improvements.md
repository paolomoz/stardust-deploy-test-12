# Improvements — apple.com

1. **[dated-pattern]** The uniform 2-up tile grid flattens the lineup into peers. Six product tiles (MacBook Air, iPad Air, Privacy, MacBook Pro, Trade In, Apple Card) all render at the same 40px/600 scale in identical 2-column rows. After the iPhone hero the eye finds no second focal point; the page reads as a 2015-era product index rather than an edited story.

2. **[ia-clutter]** Identical chrome on every tile means hierarchy rests entirely on photography. Each tile carries the same centered headline + one subhead + a "Learn more / Buy" pair over a photo, so when two tiles sit side by side (Trade In + Apple Card, both light, both service products) they compete instead of ranking.

3. **[contrast-or-density]** Benefit copy is set in the muted secondary gray (`#6e6e73`, captured at 32 hits) on the `#f5f5f7` tile fill. The one line that has to sell each product ("Now supercharged by M5.") is the lowest-contrast text on its tile. A region/consent strip (Schweiz / Weiter) also overlays the first viewport above the hero.

4. **[cliché]** The "Learn more" + "Buy" pill pair repeats on nearly every tile as undifferentiated blue pills. The convention is so uniform that the primary action (Buy) and the secondary action (Learn more) carry equal visual weight everywhere, so neither leads.

5. **[missed-opportunity]** Two captured assets are world-class and under-used on the home: the first-party product photography (3008×692 renders cropped into 1262×580 grid tiles) and SF Pro Display (held at a polite 40 to 56px single weight). The brand's cinematic and typographic range, both deployed fully on the product pages, never appears on the first surface.
